Mental Health Toolkit

Evidence-based tools drawn from CBT, ERP, and DBT to practice between sessions — plus a library of trusted resources for all kinds of mental health stuff. Not a replacement for professional therapy.

About ERP for OCD: ERP works by facing triggers and choosing not to do compulsions. Anxiety will spike — that's normal, and proof the treatment is working. The goal isn't to remove anxiety immediately; it's to learn you can tolerate it. With practice, anxiety drops on its own (habituation).

⚠ Reassurance is a compulsion too. If you find yourself checking this app to "make sure" or seeking certainty — that's OCD. Use these tools to practice sitting with uncertainty, not to neutralize it.

For BPD / dissociation: You may not remember how you felt. That's okay — log what you can, even fragments. The app remembers for you. Your feelings were real even if you forgot them.

OCD resources: International OCD Foundation (iocdf.org) · NOCD
BPD resources: NEABPD · DBT Self-Help · Linehan Institute

Thought Record

Write down a stressful thought, identify the distortion, then reframe it. For OCD: intrusive thoughts are not "you" — they're ego-dystonic, meaning they go against who you are and what you value. Reframing isn't about proving the fear wrong — it's about accepting uncertainty.

Socratic Questioning

Ask yourself these questions when a thought is bothering you. Tap a question to reveal a prompt.

Core Beliefs

Core beliefs are deep, often unspoken assumptions about yourself, others, and the world. OCD and BPD often share similar themes: fear of abandonment, "I'm broken," "I can't trust myself," "Something is wrong with me." Identifying them is the first step to changing unhelpful ones.

Behavioral Activation

When you're low, action often comes before motivation — not after. Pick one small activity to do today. Log how you feel before and after. For BPD/anhedonia or OCD-avoidance: small actions bypass the avoidance cycle. Don't wait until you "feel like it."

Problem Solving

Structured 6-step approach to work through real problems instead of spiraling. For OCD: use this when the problem is situational — not for OCD doubts. OCD thoughts can't be solved through reasoning; they require acceptance of uncertainty.

ERP Hierarchy (Exposure Ladder)

List OCD triggers and obsessions, rate SUDS (Subjective Units of Distress, 0-100), then face them from lowest to highest while not doing compulsions. Work with your therapist on higher rungs.

50

Coping Cards

Write coping statements to read when you're distressed. Keep them on your phone or print them. For OCD: avoid statements that seek certainty or neutralize the fear — the goal is acceptance, not reassurance.

Good OCD coping: "Maybe, maybe not. I can handle the uncertainty."
Avoid (reassurance): "You would never do that. You're a good person."

Obsession & Worry Tracker

Track intrusive thoughts and worries over time. For OCD: the goal is not to check whether they came true (that becomes a compulsion). The goal is to log them and practice letting go of certainty.

Reality Grounding Check-In

Dissociation can make you feel unreal, disconnected, or unsure you existed moments ago. Use this when you "come to" to anchor yourself and log what you can remember — even fragments are valid. You were real the whole time. You are real now.

Emotion-Intensity Tracker

BPD emotions can spike fast and drop fast — but dissociation can wipe the memory of the spike. Logging intensity over the day builds a record you can review to validate what you actually felt. Pattern: emotions are real, even if you forgot them.

5

DBT Skills Used

DBT (Dialectical Behavior Therapy) is the gold-standard therapy for BPD. Log the skills you actually used today — even one counts. You can't always stop the emotion, but you can choose a skill.

🧘 Mindfulness

Be present, observe without judgment.

😰 Distress Tolerance

Survive the crisis without making it worse.

💔 Emotion Regulation

Change the intensity of the emotion.

🤝 Interpersonal Effectiveness

Ask for what you need / set a boundary.

Urges Log

Track specific urges (self-harm, substance use, contact someone, flee, etc.). Logging an urge doesn't mean you acted — and "resisted" is a win, even if the urge came back.
